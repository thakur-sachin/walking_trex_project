## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Keep every servo, the joint topology and the parallelogram ankles, but:
- **bring the legs closer together under the body**;
- **give the knee the range it needs to lift the foot**;
- **free the hip to roll inward**;
- **put grippy soles on narrower feet**;
- **take mass out of the head, tail and torso stack.**

On today's robot:
- The stance hip carries 0.72 of stall at every step switch because the feet are 147 mm apart.
- The gait needs about the friction the PLA soles have.
- The knee range (±45°) leaves the swing foot ~1 mm of lift, so the policy rolls the whole body 13–17° to clear the floor. The backward bend it uses is also at the foot-vs-shank contact (+39°, as-built CAD).

The concept attacks those three directly.

## 3. What stays identical

- The 8 LX-16A servos, their bus, adapter, ids and wiring scheme.
- Joint topology: torso → head, tail; torso → hip (roll, x) → femur → tibia → ankle → foot (pitch, y).
- The double-parallelogram ankle principle, with the 50 mm idler offset and 65 / 54 mm spans.
- Pi 4, BNO055 (mounted rigidly near the plate centre), the battery (CAD: TalentCell envelope; the type is to be confirmed).
- The control loop and policy interface: 8 actions, the same observations.

Joint limits change in config only. **A new policy must be trained.** v9c transfers poorly even to −10 mm narrower hips, as expected for a policy trained on one body.

## 5. Risks

- **Self-collision.** With the feet 20 mm apart, the legs touch if both hips roll ~4° inward together, or the swing foot crosses in. The simulators have no self-collision today, so it must be **enabled in training** for v2, with a foot-spacing reward band matched to the new width.
- **Packaging.** Hip servos at ±44 mm with their bodies outboard and the hip bracket clearance need CAD work on the real parts (now available). The ±44 mm figure may become 40–50 mm.
- **The forward-knee gait** is untested: the policy has to find it (T1). If the right leg is really the Main v24 variant, its knee stops at ±57°.
- **Lateral static stability** falls a little (roll pendulum time constant 0.179 → 0.167 s; double-support polygon narrower). The model says the gait gains more than this costs, but only training can confirm it.
- **Mass numbers are unmeasured.** The as-built estimate (1.23–1.51 kg) rests on assumed infill and electronics; the mass lever could be larger or smaller than shown.
- **Supply current** (leg 3, estimate): walking may need more than the 3 A pack delivers (log 8.4). Measure the bus current on the first powered tests. A higher-current supply would be an electronics change, outside the printed parts.
- **TPU soles** add compliance; the contact model needs re-checking (the 4 ms near-rigid contact).
- **A new policy is required.** Every gait-level benefit above is a prediction, to be confirmed by the training studies.
- **Statics only.** No impact, contact-switching or policy-strategy effects; while walking, the femur/tibia servos spend most of their torque accelerating their own rotors (rotor-acceleration torque 0.24–0.26 of stall against 0.31–0.33 in total), and no geometry change touches that.
