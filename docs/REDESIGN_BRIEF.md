## 3. The mechanism to keep

- **8 Hiwonder LX-16A servos.** Each is 52 g and about 45 × 25 × 36 mm (check the datasheet). They are rated 1.667 N·m stall at 7.4 V.
  - Locations: head, tail, and hip (abduction), femur and tibia on each leg.
- **The joint topology is fixed:**
  - torso → head and tail joints;
  - torso → hip (abduction, axis along x) → femur → tibia → ankle → foot on each side;
  - femur, tibia and ankle all pitch about y.
- **Ankles are passive.** A double 4-bar parallelogram keeps each foot parallel to the torso: `ankle = femur − tibia`.
  - Lower parallelogram 50 mm (idler offset) × 54 mm (femur-axis-to-tibia-axis span).
  - Upper parallelogram 50 × 65 mm.
  - The idler links are **not** in the URDF.
  - "Same mechanism" means keeping this parallelogram principle; its dimensions may change if the case is made.
- **Onboard:**
  - Raspberry Pi 4 (8 GB);
  - BNO055 IMU in the torso;
  - a 2S battery pack (7.1 V at rest), glued under the torso plate;
  - Hiwonder BusLinker-style USB servo adapter (CH340);
  - wiring.

  The torso shell (the arched cover) has negligible mass per Sachin.
- **Material:** PLA today, soles included. TPU is an option (e.g. soles). The printer type and build volume are unknown, so ask.
- **Floor:** indoor wood.

## 4. What the simulation and the robot have shown

These are the facts that should drive the design. Sources are in PROJECT_HISTORY.md.

1. **The servos are slow and dominated by their own rotor inertia.**
   - The real LX-16A reaches about 97 °/s mid-move on a 10° step, against 375 °/s in the datasheet; the fitted no-load speed is 0.46 × datasheet.
   - The fitted armature (rotor through the gearbox) is 0.021 kg·m², **4–45× the inertia of the links it drives.**
   - So making links lighter barely speeds up joints. It helps through **mass carried, centre-of-mass height and gravity torque**, not through link inertia.
2. **Torque margin is thin.**
   - Total mass is about 1.78 kg in the model, and the 1.0 kg torso is an *estimate*.
   - In simulation the servos could not hold the body up once the torso mass was above about 1.1–1.2 kg (training with torso mass ×1.3–1.5 failed to stand).
   - The hips spend 12–18% of walking steps above 80% of stall torque.
   - The head (231 g in CAD) and tail (115 g) are heavy, cantilevered links.
3. **Joint ranges limit speed.**
   - The tibias sit on their ±45° limit 81–100% of the time at the top commanded speed, and the policy walks crouched. The real ranges are not measured; ±45° (hips ±30°) is a working value.
   - Servo range is 240° (0–1000 ticks). The left femur's horn indexing leaves only ~3° spare at −45°.
   - **A redesign could deliberately give the knee/tibia more range and index the horns for it.**
4. **Lateral stability.** Torso roll swings 13–17° peak-to-peak while walking (the target is 12°). Foot spacing, foot width and centre-of-mass height affect this.
5. **Feet.**
   - Contact is flat PLA on wood, with an estimated friction μ of 0.3–0.4. Policies slip and slow down at low friction.
   - Soles could be TPU, wider or rounded at the edges (a C4 idea in the plan).
6. **The CAD torso mesh is a hollow shell authored as steel** (2.59 kg in the URDF). The simulator now models it as the shell at PLA density (0.41 kg) plus a battery/electronics box. The real mass and centre of mass have never been measured.
7. **The ankle linkage carries foot torque into the torso**, not into the servos. In simulation the foot moment was a significant load (0.15–0.18 of stall on average).

## 5. What exists as data

- `assets/robots/trex/meshes/*.stl`: **one merged mesh per URDF link** (11 links: base_link, head, tail, femur/tibia/ankle/foot × 2).
  - These are simulation link meshes, not separated printable parts.
  - Servos, horns, fasteners and the 4-bar idler links are not individually available; the idlers are absent entirely.
  - Units are mm, scaled by 0.001 in the URDF.
- `assets/robots/trex/Trex.urdf`: link masses and inertias from Fusion. Every link except the torso is about 1.06 g/cm³ plastic. Joint origins and axes are as exported.
- `assets/robots/trex/servo_logs/`: real servo response logs.
- Photos and videos of the robot, including the 4-bar mechanism: `~/walking_trex_project/media/`.

**Sachin has offered to export parts from Fusion 360.** If the merged link meshes are not enough (likely for a printable redesign), ask for exactly what you need. Probable needs:

## 6. Your first leg (stop and report at the end)

1. **Audit the current design from the data in hand.**
   - Per link: mass properties from the meshes versus the URDF, centre of mass, overall centre-of-mass height standing, foot geometry and support polygon, and leg kinematics (stride and foot clearance within the joint ranges).
   - Identify mass that does not need to be there, and geometry that limits range or causes collisions.
2. **Quantify what matters, with simulation evidence, on CPU only.**
   - Use the MuJoCo model with the fitted servo model to run sensitivity studies on the candidate design levers:
     - torso mass and centre-of-mass height/position;
     - head and tail mass and length;
     - leg segment lengths;
     - foot size and sole friction;
     - tibia/femur range;
     - hip spacing.
   - Use static and quasi-static analysis where it fits: gravity torque per servo at the standing and crouched poses, torque margin, support polygon, roll stability. Optionally run short scripted or open-loop gaits.
   - **Do not train policies.** Training needs the GPU, which is busy; propose the training studies instead.
   - Rank the levers by expected effect on walking: torque margin, speed, stability, robustness.
3. **Propose a redesign concept for Sachin to approve.**
   - Cover what changes per part, why (tied to the evidence), the expected numbers, risks, and what stays identical (servos, electronics, mechanism).
   - Include a **precise list of the CAD data needed from Sachin's Fusion model** and the print/material questions.
   - No detailed CAD before approval, unless something is clearly useful and cheap, such as a parametric foot.
4. Write it all to `docs/REDESIGN_LOG.md` (your running log, in the style of PROJECT_HISTORY.md) and `docs/REDESIGN_CONCEPT.md` (the proposal). Commit on `redesign`.
